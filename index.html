MASTER THESIS2025 – 2026

Multi-Agent Systems for Image Analysis: Object and Complex Scene Detection

Universität Hildesheim · Supervisors: Prof. Dr. Thomas Mandl, Shereen Elsayed

When can an automatic answer about a historical illustration be trusted without a person checking it? I built a multi-agent pipeline over the Colibri archive (12,110 illustrations from historical books): a retrained YOLOv11m object finder, a zero-shot SigLIP scene matcher and a LLaVA-OneVision 7B describer, mapped to probabilities by learned models and routed by conformal risk control — approve, short-list, or send to a person.

76.4%approved automatically at a 5% risk promise · original design: 63.0%
0.903mean AUROC over 8 object classes · single-model probe: 0.838
  • Audited the original design and showed its “disagreement” score was confidence in disguise. In a test fixed in advance, agent disagreement ranked errors worse than chance (AUROC 0.378) while the system’s own confidence reached 0.825 — on 149 of 199 errors, all three agents were wrong together.
  • Showed that a detector graded against its teacher’s labels looks good (mAP50 0.768) but scores 0.146 against human boxes; a better teacher (Grounding DINO) lifted it from 0.123 to 0.210.
  • Designed the evaluation: 1,250 labelled pictures from 550 books, book-disjoint 60/20/20 splits redrawn 200 times, key tests written down before running, and four baselines (original system, SigLIP linear probe, best single agent, zero-shot).
  • Tested the risk promise on random archive pictures: calibrating on the curated pool let 16.2% wrong approvals through; calibrating on a random archive sample under one scene definition brought it to 5.4–5.9%.
